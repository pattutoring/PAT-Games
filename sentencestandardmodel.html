SENTENCE STANDARD MODEL

Meaning interacts. Syntax expresses.

A sentence is not treated as the primitive object. It is the visible syntactic expression of a deeper relational interaction: lexical states enter a field, exchange semantic and syntactic carriers, transform through conjugation, and collapse into sentence form.

THE MODEL

From Meaning Interaction to Sentence Form

The Sentence Standard Model separates underlying relational meaning from its charged syntactic realization. Both semantics and syntax exist at every scale, but the model lets us move from transformations of meaning into the linguistic objects and interactions that express them.

Context / Higgs Field Context selects which possibilities acquire effective semantic relevance or mass.
Semantic Mass A lexical state can carry different amounts of relational information depending upon the field around it.
Syntactic Charge Grammar constrains how an expression may attach, agree and interact.
Conservation Expression can transform while preserving the underlying semantic relation.
Reversibility A visible sentence can be decomposed back into the interactions that produced it.
Scale Meaning-level transformations resolve downward into linguistic particles, operators and vertices.
